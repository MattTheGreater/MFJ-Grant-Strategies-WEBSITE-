FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

Straight answers on fees, timelines, guarantees, and what the numbers on this site mean. If your question is not here, ask it directly.

Working with MFJ
How is a grants consultant different from a grant writer?+
A grant writer produces a document. A consultant decides whether you should apply at all, which funder fits, how to position you, how to manage the award after you win, and how to build the internal capacity to sustain funding over time. Writing is one step in a much larger strategy, and usually not the step that determines whether you win.
Who does MFJ work with?+
Tribal nations and tribally owned organizations, municipal, county, and state governments, universities and research institutions, nonprofits, healthcare organizations, housing and community development organizations, and international NGOs. Federal funding is the specialty, not the limit.
Where is MFJ located, and do you work remotely?+
MFJ Grant Strategies is based in Richmond, Virginia, and serves clients nationwide. Most engagements are conducted remotely, with on-site work where an engagement calls for it.
What is a fractional grants department?+
An outsourced, part-time grants office on a monthly retainer. Instead of hiring a full-time grants manager, your organization retains a specialist to run funding research, proposal development, award administration, 2 CFR 200 compliance, reporting, and staff training. See the fractional grants department page for scope and cost.
Fees
Do you charge commission-based or contingency fees?+
No. We never tie our fees for finding funding or developing proposals to whether you win. Prospecting, review, and writing are billed as flat project fees or a monthly retainer, the same whether or not the award lands. Contingency-based grant-writing fees run counter to the ethical standards of the Association of Fundraising Professionals, because they reward volume over fit.
Can our grant pay for your work?+
Ongoing administration of an active portfolio is a separate engagement, billed on its own terms, and where appropriate it can be supported through an award's indirect-cost budget. That is payment for administering funds you have already won, not a commission on winning them. Pre-award work is generally not chargeable to a federal award.
What does a fractional grants department cost compared to a hire?+
A full-time grants manager typically costs $65K to $105K a year in salary, benefits, and overhead. A fractional arrangement is a monthly retainer sized to the portfolio. We quote it after an inventory of your awards and pipeline.
Results and guarantees
What is your success rate?+
MFJ does not publish a client win rate. What matters more than a headline number is that we only pursue funding where you are genuinely competitive. If we ever publish a rate, we will disclose the period, the denominator, how pending applications are treated, and which engagements qualify.
Can you guarantee grant awards?+
No one ethically can. Funding decisions rest with the funder. What we control is fit, positioning, and execution. We only pursue opportunities where you are genuinely competitive, which is why selectivity matters more than any promise.
Can you guarantee a clean audit?+
No. Audit outcomes rest with the auditor and depend on the organization's records and practices over the whole period. What we can do is build the systems, policies, and records trail that make a clean audit the expected result rather than a hope.
What do the portfolio figures on your site mean?+
They are funds administered, managed, or supported in the founder's prior roles at USAID, Virginia Commonwealth University, and the Oneida Indian Nation. They are not funds awarded to him, not funds raised by MFJ, and not a win rate. The founder page has a table stating exactly what each figure represents and what it does not.
Timelines and process
How long does it take to secure funding?+
It depends on the funder and the cycle. Federal windows can run several months from application to award; state and local programs often move faster. We map realistic timelines during the readiness assessment so you can plan around them rather than chase.
How far ahead should we start a federal application?+
Registrations such as SAM.gov and organizational approvals should be in place before the notice of funding opportunity is published. For the application itself, most organizations need six to eight weeks from a decision to apply to a confident submission.
Do you provide grant management after an award?+
Yes. Post-award is where many organizations stumble. We handle 2 CFR 200 compliance, reporting, reconciliation, subrecipient monitoring, and audit readiness through closeout.
What happens in the first 90 days of a retainer?+
Days one to thirty are an inventory and readiness assessment of your current awards, obligations, and pipeline. Days thirty-one to sixty set up the reporting calendar, compliance routines, and documentation systems. Days sixty-one to ninety move into active pursuit and administration, with your staff trained on the routines as they are built.
2 CFR 200 and compliance
What is 2 CFR 200?+
The Uniform Guidance: the federal regulation that sets the administrative requirements, cost principles, and audit requirements for federal awards. Nearly every federal grant incorporates it by reference. The Knowledge Center has a complete guide for nonprofit organizations.
Do we need a Single Audit?+
If your organization expended federal awards above the current threshold in 2 CFR 200.501 during its fiscal year, yes. The threshold changed in the 2024 revision of the regulation, so confirm the current figure in the regulation rather than relying on a remembered number.
Is the Single Audit Readiness Scorecard an audit?+
No. It is a free twelve-question self-assessment of internal controls against the areas a Single Audit examines. It is illustrative, not an audit, and not a substitute for one.
