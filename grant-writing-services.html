What grant writing includes

More than words on a page.

Proposal narrative

A clear, evidence-backed case written in the funder's order, against the funder's criteria, with local data instead of generic claims.

Budget & budget narrative

A budget built from the plan, with every line traceable, an indirect rate with a basis, and arithmetic that foots.

Funding research

Finding the right opportunity in the first place, matched to what your organization can genuinely win, not a spray-and-pray list.

Review against the rubric

An internal read of the draft against the published scoring criteria before it is submitted, the way a review panel will read it.

Registrations & submission

SAM.gov, Grants.gov, and the organizational approvals that sink applications when they start too late.

A plan for after the award

Proposals that show the funder you can administer the money, which protects the award you worked to win.

Grant writer vs. grant consultant

Writing is the step most people see. It is rarely the step that wins.

Plenty of people advertise grant writing services and will write whatever application you point them at. That solves one document. It does not decide whether you should have applied, whether the funder was the right fit, whether the budget will survive scrutiny, or who administers the award once it lands.

MFJ writes grants, but as part of the whole picture: strategy, positioning, the proposal itself, and the administration that follows. If all you need is a writer for a single application, we can do that. More often, organizations discover the writing was never the real problem, and that is the conversation worth having first.

Grants, not general fundraising

A word on what this is, and is not.

Grant funding and fundraising overlap, but they are not the same work. MFJ concentrates on grant funding: federal, state, local, and private foundation grants, from finding the opportunity through administering the award. That includes the funding research and funding strategy that decide where your effort goes.

We are not a general fundraising shop running annual campaigns, major-gift programs, or capital campaigns. Those are real disciplines with their own specialists. If grants are your target, this is exactly our work. If you need broad development operations, we will tell you honestly and point you toward the right kind of help.

FAQ

Common questions

What is the difference between a grant writer and a grant consultant?+
A grant writer produces the application document. A grant consultant decides with you whether to apply at all, which funder fits, how to position the organization, how to build a budget that survives review, and how to administer the award afterward. MFJ does the writing, but as one part of that larger strategy, which is usually what actually decides whether you win.
Do you write grants for nonprofits?+
Yes. Nonprofit grant writing is a core part of the practice: federal, state, local, and private foundation proposals, with the budget and narrative built to the funder's scoring criteria. We also help nonprofits build a diversified funding base so the organization is not dependent on any single source.
Do you write federal grant applications?+
Yes, and federal is the specialty. Federal proposals are written to the notice of funding opportunity and its review criteria, with a budget that matches the plan and an administrative approach that shows the agency you can manage the award once you win it.
Do you do fundraising, or only grants?+
MFJ focuses on grant funding: federal, state, local, and foundation grants. We are not a general fundraising or development firm running donor campaigns, major gifts, or capital campaigns. Grant strategy complements that work, but if you need broad fundraising operations, we are not the right fit and we will say so.
Do you charge a percentage of the grant?+
Never. Grant writing and proposal development are billed as flat project fees or a monthly retainer, the same whether or not the award lands. Contingency-based grant-writing fees run counter to the ethical standards of the Association of Fundraising Professionals, because they reward volume over fit.
Can you write grants for governments and tribal nations?+
Yes. Government grant writing services and tribal grant writing are both part of the practice, informed by funder-side federal experience and a grants role with a tribal government. See the tribal and municipal pages for how those engagements work.

Need a grant written, or a second opinion on whether to apply?

Tell us about the opportunity and your organization. The first conversation is about fit, and we will say so if we are not the right choice.
