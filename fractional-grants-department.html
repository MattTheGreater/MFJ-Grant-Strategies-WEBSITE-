What is a fractional grants department?

A fractional grants department is an outsourced, part-time grants office. Instead of hiring a full-time grants manager, your organization retains a specialist on a monthly basis to run funding research, proposal development, award administration, 2 CFR 200 compliance, reporting, and staff training, at a fraction of the cost of a salaried position. It is the grants department you do not have in-house.

Who it is for

The mid-sized organization problem.

You are too large to qualify for grassroots micro-grants, yet too small to justify a full-time in-house grants team. The result is a funding dead zone: aware of opportunities, but without the capacity to pursue them competitively, and without anyone who owns the awards once they land.

Most organizations in that position hire a grant writer for a single application. That solves one proposal. It does not solve the pipeline, the reporting calendar, the subrecipient monitoring, or the audit that follows. A fractional grants department does.

Scope

What a fractional grants department covers.

Funding research

Curated prospecting aligned to your priorities and genuine funder intent, not a spray-and-pray list.

Proposal development

Narrative and budget built to the rubric the scoring panel will actually use.

Award administration

Award setup, drawdowns, financial and programmatic reporting through closeout.

Compliance oversight

2 CFR 200 adherence, procurement standards, cost principles, subrecipient monitoring.

Reporting & audit readiness

SF-425 and funder reports on cycle, with the records trail a Single Audit expects.

Staff training & systems

Internal grants-management routines your team can run, so capacity stays when we go.

No contingency fees

Prospecting, proposal development, and application review are billed as flat project fees or a monthly retainer, the same whether or not the award comes through. We never tie our fee to whether you win. Contingency-based grant fees run counter to the ethical standards of the Association of Fundraising Professionals, because they reward volume over fit.

Ongoing administration of an active portfolio is a separate engagement, billed on its own terms.

The first 90 days

What you get, and when.

  • Days 1 to 30. Inventory and readiness. Every current award, obligation, deadline, and open item catalogued. An honest readiness assessment of your pipeline and your administrative systems.
  • Days 31 to 60. Systems and calendar. A single reporting and compliance calendar across all awards. Procurement, allowability, and subrecipient-monitoring routines written to match how you actually operate.
  • Days 61 to 90. Active pursuit and administration. Prioritized opportunities in motion, reporting running on cycle, and your staff trained on each routine as it is built.
Ideal client

Who this works best for.

A fractional arrangement fits an organization managing, or about to manage, several federal, state, or foundation awards at once, with no one on staff who has administered a federal award before, and leadership that wants a funding base it can plan around rather than a one-time win.

It is not a fit for an organization looking for the cheapest possible writer for a single application, or for one that wants to pay only if it wins. Those are legitimate needs; they are just not what this is.

FAQ

Common questions

When does an organization need a fractional grants department?+
When it is too large to get by on one-off grant writing but too small to justify a full-time grants team. The typical sign is an organization that wins occasional awards and then struggles to administer them, or leaves funding on the table because no one owns the pipeline.
What does it cost?+
A full-time grants manager typically costs $65K to $105K a year in salary, benefits, and overhead. A fractional arrangement is billed as a monthly retainer sized to the portfolio, and ongoing award administration can often be supported through an award's indirect-cost budget. We never bill on contingency.
Can our own staff still be involved?+
They should be. The point is to build your institutional capacity, not replace it. We train your team on every routine we build so the operation keeps running after the engagement ends.
How is this different from hiring a grant writer?+
A grant writer produces a document for one application. A fractional grants department owns the whole function: strategy, pipeline, proposals, administration, compliance, and reporting, month after month, across every award.
