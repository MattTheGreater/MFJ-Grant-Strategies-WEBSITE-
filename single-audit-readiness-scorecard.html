Single Audit Readiness Scorecard

Twelve questions on the internal-control areas a Single Audit under 2 CFR 200 Subpart F examines. Answer honestly, score instantly, and see where to shore up before an auditor looks.

What this is, and is not. An illustrative self-assessment of your readiness, written by MFJ from the control areas auditors examine most. It is not an audit, not legal or accounting advice, and it does not replace a compliance review against the current regulation. A non-federal entity that expends federal awards at or above the current Single Audit threshold in a fiscal year is generally required to have one; confirm the current threshold against 2 CFR 200 rather than an older figure.
1 · Written policies
Do you have written, board- or leadership-approved policies covering federal award administration, procurement, allowable costs, and conflicts of interest?
2 · Procurement
Does every federally funded purchase follow a documented procurement method matched to its dollar amount, with the rationale and any competition on file?
3 · Allowability
Before a cost is charged to a federal award, does someone check it is allowable, allocable, and reasonable under the cost principles and the award terms, and document that check?
4 · Cash management
Are federal drawdowns timed to actual disbursement needs, with the time between drawdown and payment minimized and documented?
5 · Time and effort
Is personnel time charged to federal awards supported by records that reflect actual effort, reviewed and certified on a regular cycle?
6 · Subrecipient monitoring
If you pass federal funds to other organizations, have you documented the subrecipient-versus-contractor determination, assessed each subrecipient's risk, and monitored them accordingly?
7 · Reporting calendar
Is there one calendar of every financial and programmatic report due across all federal awards, with an owner for each, and were last year's reports filed on time?
8 · Reconciliation
Do reported federal expenditures reconcile to your general ledger, award by award, before each report is submitted?
9 · Schedule of expenditures
Can you produce, today, a complete schedule of federal expenditures by award and assistance listing for the current fiscal year?
10 · Records retention
Are award documents, procurement files, cost support, and reports retained in a known location for the period your awards require, and retrievable on request?
11 · Prior findings
If a prior audit or monitoring visit produced findings, has each been resolved with a documented corrective action?
12 · Indirect costs
Is the indirect cost rate you charge to federal awards supported by a current negotiated rate agreement or a documented election of the de minimis rate, applied consistently?
