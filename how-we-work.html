Our process

A complete grant strategy, not just writing.

Every MFJ engagement moves through four phases: readiness, research, proposal, and portfolio management. Not every client needs all four. An organization with a strong pipeline and a weak back office may start at phase four; one with no federal history starts at phase one. What never changes is the order of operations: an honest assessment comes before a dollar is spent pursuing anything, and administration is planned before the award arrives.
The four phases

What happens, and what you receive.

1

Strategic Funding Readiness Assessment

Before we pursue anything, we need to know where your organization actually stands. We evaluate organizational capacity, financial health, program outcomes, and documentation, and give you an honest picture of what you are ready to compete for, and what to fix first.

  • Readiness report with gaps named plainly
  • Priority program identification
  • Documentation checklist for registrations, policies, and approvals

2

Grant Research & Prospecting

Curated funder research aligned to your priorities and genuine funder intent, not a spray-and-pray list. We identify where you are competitive, where you are not, and what the calendar looks like across agencies.

  • Prioritized prospect list with fit and odds stated
  • Application calendar starting from registrations and approvals

3

Proposal Development & Submission

Narrative and budget built across federal programs plus state and local streams, written to the rubric the scoring panel will actually use, with the budget telling the same story as the plan.

  • Complete application package with budget narrative
  • Internal review against the published criteria before submission

4

Active Portfolio Management

Risk management and regulatory compliance at the core: 2 CFR 200 adherence, financial reporting, reconciliation, subrecipient monitoring, and audit readiness through closeout. The fractional grants department you do not have in-house.

  • One reporting and compliance calendar across every award
  • Written policies that match how you actually operate
  • Staff trained on each routine as it is built

Fees

How we charge, and how we do not.

Flat fees or retainer

Projects are quoted as a flat fee for a defined deliverable. Ongoing work is a monthly retainer sized to the portfolio.

Never contingency

We never tie a fee for finding funding or developing a proposal to whether you win. Contingency fees reward volume over fit.

Indirect cost recovery

Where appropriate, administering an active portfolio can be supported through an award's indirect-cost budget. That is payment for administering funds already won.

One grant is impact. A portfolio is transformation.

Most organizations chase grants one at a time, in isolation. We approach funding like an ecosystem: individual awards designed to support and sustain one another, building lasting institutional capacity rather than waste or dependency.

That is also why we are selective. The clients we do our best work with are building something that lasts.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you charge commission or contingency fees?+
No. Prospecting, review, and proposal work are billed as flat project fees or a monthly retainer, the same whether or not the award lands. Contingency-based grant fees run counter to the ethical standards of the Association of Fundraising Professionals. Ongoing administration of an active portfolio is a separate engagement, billed on its own terms.
Will you tell us not to apply?+
Yes, and often. The readiness assessment exists to give an honest read on fit and odds before you spend money pursuing an opportunity. Selectivity is the strategy.
Can you work alongside our existing grants staff?+
That is the preferred arrangement. We build routines your team runs and train them on each one, so the capacity stays after the engagement ends.
Do you guarantee awards or compliance outcomes?+
No one ethically can. Funding decisions rest with the funder and audit outcomes with the auditor. What we control is fit, positioning, execution, and the quality of the systems and records behind an award.
