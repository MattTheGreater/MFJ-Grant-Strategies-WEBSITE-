The problem

Grant writing is only 20% of the work.

The other 80% is strategy: identifying the right funders, reading their genuine intent, positioning the organization compellingly, and timing the pursuit to funder cycles. Organizations that skip the strategy end up with a spray-and-pray list of long shots, or a single large grant they cannot plan around.

The result is familiar: a loss no one can explain, or a win that quietly creates dependency on the next cycle. Neither builds the durable capacity that makes an organization stronger over time.

Our approach

Funding as an ecosystem, not a lottery.

  • Identify real opportunities. Curated funder research aligned to your priorities and genuine funder intent, not a long list of long shots.
  • Read funder intent. Strategy informed by how funders actually decide, from someone who has sat in the funder's chair.
  • Build the portfolio. Cross-agency coordination so awards reinforce one another rather than compete for the same capacity.
  • Time the pursuit. A calendar aligned to budget cycles and announcements, so you are positioned before a window opens.
  • Plan for sustainability. A base designed to last, so no single source can leave a gap when it shifts.

FAQ

Common questions

What is a federal funding strategy?+
A deliberate plan for which opportunities to pursue, when, and why, built around an organization's priorities and the genuine intent of each funder. It replaces chasing grants one at a time with a coordinated portfolio that builds over time.
How is strategy different from grant writing?+
Grant writing produces an application. Strategy decides whether to apply at all, which funder fits, how to position the organization, and how awards fit together into a portfolio. Writing is one step; strategy is what usually determines whether you win and whether the funding lasts.
Who benefits most from a funding strategy?+
Organizations that want a durable funding base rather than a one-time award: governments, tribal nations, universities, healthcare organizations, and nonprofits building something meant to last. Strategy is where a scattered set of grants becomes a coherent, sustainable portfolio.
