What a consultant does

More than a grant writer.

A grant writer produces a document. A grants consultant decides with you whether to apply at all, which program fits, how to position the organization against the competition, how to build a budget that survives the agency's review, and how to run the award after the letter arrives. Writing is one step, and usually not the step that decides the outcome.

That matters most for organizations in the middle: too large for one-off grant writing, too small for a full-time grants office. They win occasional awards and then struggle to administer them, or leave funding unpursued because no one owns the pipeline. MFJ fills that gap, either project by project or as a fractional grants department on a monthly retainer.

Federal and state

Two kinds of money, one set of habits.

Federal grants

Direct awards from agencies such as HUD, DOT, DOJ, USDA, EPA, HHS, BIA, and IHS. Governed by 2 CFR 200, the agency's supplement, and the program statute. Competitive cycles run months; administration runs years.

State pass-through grants

Most state grants to local governments, tribes, and nonprofits are federal dollars passed through a state agency. The state adds its own terms, but 2 CFR 200 still applies, and the state is required to monitor you as its subrecipient.

State-funded programs

Programs paid from state appropriations follow the state's own rules rather than the federal ones. Faster cycles, different reporting, and often a different agency contact. Knowing which kind of award you hold decides which rulebook governs it.

What stays the same

Fit before application. A budget built from the plan. Written policies. One calendar of every deadline. Documentation made at the time, not reconstructed later. The habits that keep a federal award clean keep a state award clean too.

FAQ

Common questions

What does a federal and state grants consultant do?+
Decides with you whether to apply at all, which funder and program fit, how to position the organization, how to build the application and budget to the scoring criteria, and then how to administer the award under the funder's rules once it lands. Writing the proposal is one step; most of the value is in the decisions before and the administration after.
How is a state grant different from a federal one?+
Most state grants to local governments, tribes, and nonprofits are federal money passed through a state agency, so 2 CFR 200 still applies alongside the state's own terms. Some programs are funded purely with state appropriations and follow the state's rules instead. Knowing which kind you hold decides which rulebook governs the award.
Do you only work in Virginia?+
No. MFJ is based in Richmond, Virginia, and serves clients nationwide. Federal rules are the same in every state, and most engagements are conducted remotely.
Do you charge a percentage of the grant?+
Never. Prospecting, review, and proposal work are billed as flat project fees or a monthly retainer, the same whether or not the award lands. Ongoing administration of an active portfolio is a separate engagement and, where appropriate, can be supported through an award's indirect-cost budget.
What size organization do you work with?+
Organizations too large for one-off grant writing and too small for a full-time grants office: tribal nations, cities and counties, state agencies, universities, healthcare organizations, and nonprofits managing or pursuing several awards at once.
