The problem

The rules are not optional, and they are not obvious.

2 CFR 200 sets the administrative requirements, cost principles, and audit requirements for federal awards. It decides what costs are allowable, how procurement must run, how subrecipients are monitored, and what a Single Audit will examine. The language is dense, the thresholds change, and the consequences of getting it wrong land years later as questioned costs or findings.

Many organizations only discover a compliance gap when an auditor finds it. By then the award is spent and the finding is on the record. The better time to get this right is before the first dollar moves.

Our approach

Compliance as a system, not a scramble.

  • Read the current rule. We work from the regulation as it stands today, including proposed changes to the Uniform Guidance, not from a figure copied off an old award.
  • Map your exposure. A plain-language review of where your organization is and is not meeting 2 CFR 200, with the gaps named honestly.
  • Build the controls. Written policies for procurement, cost allocation, cash management, and subrecipient monitoring that match how your organization actually operates.
  • Prepare for the Single Audit. Documentation, internal controls, and a records trail that make the audit a confirmation rather than a surprise.

FAQ

Common questions

What is 2 CFR 200?+
2 CFR 200, the Uniform Guidance, is the federal regulation that governs how recipients administer federal awards. It covers administrative requirements, cost principles, and audit requirements, including the Single Audit. Nearly every federal grant recipient is subject to it.
Who has to comply with the Uniform Guidance?+
Non-federal entities that receive federal awards, including state and local governments, tribal governments, universities, hospitals, and nonprofits, along with the subrecipients they pass funds to. The specifics vary by entity type and award, which is why a tailored review matters.
What triggers a Single Audit?+
A non-federal entity that expends a federal award amount at or above the current Single Audit threshold in a fiscal year is generally required to have a Single Audit. Because that threshold is set by regulation and changes over time, we confirm the current figure against the regulation rather than relying on an older number.
