The problem

The 80% of the work that happens after you win.

Most organizations put their energy into the application and treat the award as the reward. In practice, writing the proposal is a fraction of the effort. The larger and riskier part is administering the award correctly once the money arrives.

Federal awards carry rules on how funds are drawn down, how costs are documented, how procurement is run, how subrecipients are monitored, and how everything is reported and retained. Miss those rules and a win can turn into questioned costs, audit findings, or repayment. Mid-sized organizations feel this most acutely, because they are too large for a single-grant workaround and too small to staff a full grants office.

Our approach

Run the award like the funder expects it to be run.

  • Set it up right. Award terms translated into your chart of accounts, budget, and reporting calendar before the first drawdown.
  • Keep the money clean. Drawdowns, cash management, cost allocation, and cost share tracked so every dollar ties back to an allowable, documented purpose.
  • Report on time, every time. Financial and programmatic reports, including the SF-425, prepared, reconciled, and filed to the cycle each award demands.
  • Watch the subrecipients. Risk-based subrecipient monitoring that catches problems downstream before they become your finding.
  • Stay audit-ready. Records, internal controls, and documentation maintained so a Single Audit is a review, not a scramble.

In practice

A typical engagement.

Example scenario

A mid-sized nonprofit wins its first seven-figure federal award and has no one in-house who has administered one. We set up the award in their accounting system, build a reporting calendar, train their finance lead on drawdowns and allowable costs, stand up subrecipient agreements and monitoring, and carry the reporting through the first full year, so the organization keeps the award in good standing and builds the internal muscle to run the next one. This is an illustrative example of the kind of work we do, not a specific client account.

FAQ

Common questions

What does federal grants management include?+
Everything after the award letter: setting up the award in your financial system, drawdowns and cash management, financial and programmatic reporting such as the SF-425, procurement that meets federal standards, cost-share tracking, subrecipient monitoring, records retention, and closeout.
Do you manage federal awards on an ongoing basis?+
Yes. We can serve as your fractional grants department, administering an active portfolio month to month, or support your internal team on specific awards. Ongoing administration is billed on its own terms and, where appropriate, can be supported through an award's indirect-cost budget.
What happens if a federal grant is mismanaged?+
Poorly administered awards risk questioned costs, disallowed costs, Single Audit findings, repayment, and in serious cases suspension or debarment. Sound post-award systems built around 2 CFR 200 keep funding you have already won from becoming a liability.
