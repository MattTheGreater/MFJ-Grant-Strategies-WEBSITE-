The problem

Most applicants only ever see the award looking up.

Organizations that pursue federal assistance usually experience it from one direction: as applicants submitting into a process they cannot see inside. They guess at what the agency wants, how proposals are weighed, and what administration the funder will expect once the award lands.

Implementing organizations and NGOs carry real compliance weight, including subrecipient monitoring and award administration, often across difficult operating environments. Without insight into funder logic, that weight is heavier than it needs to be.

Our approach

Funder logic first, recipient reality on top.

  • Write to the award. Proposals and work plans shaped around how a funder develops and judges an assistance award.
  • Monitor the subrecipients. Risk-based monitoring and agreements that hold up when funds pass through to partners.
  • Administer cleanly. Reporting, documentation, and cost management that match what the agency expects after award.
  • Prepare for scrutiny. Records and controls built for the review that federal assistance invites.

FAQ

Common questions

What is a federal assistance award?+
A federal assistance award, such as a grant or cooperative agreement, transfers funding to carry out a public purpose rather than to buy goods or services for the government. Agencies like USAID use assistance instruments to fund development and humanitarian work through implementing organizations.
How does funder-side experience help a recipient?+
Someone who has obligated funds on the agency side knows how awards are developed, what a funder watches for, and how decisions and documentation are judged. Applied to the recipient side, that means proposals and administration shaped the way the funder actually reads them.
Do you work with international NGOs?+
Yes. International NGOs and development organizations that receive federal assistance face award administration, subrecipient monitoring, and compliance obligations much like domestic recipients. We help them manage those obligations across the award lifecycle.
