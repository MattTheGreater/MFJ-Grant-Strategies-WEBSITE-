The problem

Funding scattered across departments and cycles.

Local governments pursue federal funding through public works, housing, public safety, and health, each department on its own clock, each award reporting separately. Opportunities move on budget cycles and program announcements that are easy to miss. The result is a portfolio no one sees as a whole, and compliance risk that surfaces only at audit.

A city or county does not usually need a bigger grants office. It needs coordination across the one it has, and senior help on the awards that carry the most risk.

Our approach

Coordinate the portfolio, then run it clean.

  • One funding picture. A cross-agency strategy that leadership can see and plan around, not a scramble department by department.
  • Position before the window. Tracking budget cycles and announcements so you are ready when a program opens, not scrambling after it closes.
  • Competitive applications. Proposals built to the scoring rubric for the federal programs that fit local priorities.
  • Clean administration. Reporting, procurement, and subrecipient monitoring handled to federal standards across departments.
  • Audit readiness. Internal controls and documentation that make the Single Audit a confirmation.

A county manages federal awards across four departments with no shared calendar, and leadership cannot see the whole portfolio. We inventory the awards, build one compliance calendar, standardize reporting and procurement practices against federal rules, and prepare the records for the Single Audit, so leadership sees its federal funding as one coordinated program and the audit goes smoothly. FAQ

Common questions

What does a municipal grant consultant do?+
A municipal grant consultant helps a city, county, or local agency build a federal funding strategy, coordinate grants across multiple agencies, develop competitive applications, and administer the awards in compliance with federal rules, including through audit and closeout.
How do local governments manage grants across departments?+
Larger local governments often have awards spread across public works, housing, public safety, and health departments, each reporting on its own. A coordinated approach aligns the calendar, standardizes compliance, and gives leadership one view of the whole funding portfolio.
Do you help with audit readiness for local governments?+
Yes. Local governments that expend federal funds at or above the current Single Audit threshold are generally subject to a Single Audit. We maintain the internal controls, documentation, and records trail so the audit confirms a compliant program.
