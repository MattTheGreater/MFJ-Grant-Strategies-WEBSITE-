Biography

From the funder's chair to the community.

Matt Johnston's journey in public funding began in a U.S. foreign-service family, living in five countries before he ever filed a grant report. Watching American dollars flow into community projects around the world, he learned early that some funding builds things that last, some is wasted, and some quietly creates dependency.

He began his federal career in the grants unit of the U.S. Agency for International Development, where he obligated roughly $305 million across approximately 320 awards, learning from the inside exactly how a federal funder thinks, decides, and protects taxpayer dollars. That included drafting awards to implementing organizations doing humanitarian and international development work.

From there he crossed to the recipient side: an $86 million annual research portfolio at Virginia Commonwealth University's College of Engineering, an R1 institution, and a $35 million federal portfolio with the Oneida Indian Nation, America's First Ally. He has also served as an independent federal grant reviewer, scoring applications against the rubrics funders actually use.

Now based in Richmond, Virginia, and serving clients nationwide, he founded MFJ Grant Strategies in 2025 to bring that full-circle perspective to the governments, tribal nations, universities, healthcare organizations, and nonprofits doing real development work in their own communities. Federal is the specialty, not the limit, with one aim throughout: building institutional capacity that outlasts any single grant.

That conviction runs in the family. Matt is the great-great nephew of the philanthropist Martha Berry, who built Berry College to lift rural communities through self-sufficiency rather than charity.

How we work

Three commitments.

No contingency fees

Prospecting, review, and proposal work are billed as flat fees or a retainer, the same whether or not the award lands.

Selective by design

We turn down opportunities we do not think you can win. That is why the work we take on performs.

Capacity, not dependency

The goal is an organization that can win and manage funding itself, long after we are gone.

Let's see if we're the right fit.

Initial consultations are used to evaluate fit. We only take on engagements where we believe we can make a meaningful difference.
