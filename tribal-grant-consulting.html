The problem

Funding layered across agencies, on a government's terms.

Tribal governments pursue funding from the BIA, IHS, HUD, and many other agencies at once, each with its own rules, cycles, and reporting. The opportunity is real, but so is the administrative load, and it falls on departments that are often already stretched. Self-governance options like 638 contracting add control and add obligations in the same step.

What a tribe needs is not a vendor who treats it like any other applicant, but a partner who understands both the federal machinery and the sovereignty of the government receiving the funds.

Our approach

Coordinate the portfolio, respect the sovereignty.

  • One portfolio, many agencies. Cross-agency funding strategy that coordinates BIA, IHS, HUD, and other streams instead of chasing them one grant at a time.
  • 638 and self-governance. Support for ISDEAA contracting and compacting, including the administration those arrangements require.
  • Set-asides and eligibility. Positioning for tribal set-asides and program eligibility so the tribe competes where it is strongest.
  • Compliance that fits. Uniform Guidance and program compliance handled in a way that respects the government's structure and decision-making.
  • Capacity that stays. Systems and training that build the tribe's own grants capacity, rather than long-term dependence on a consultant.

FAQ

Common questions

What is 638 contracting?+
Under the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act (ISDEAA, Public Law 93-638), a tribe can contract or compact to operate programs that a federal agency such as the BIA or IHS would otherwise run. It gives tribes direct control over federal programs and funding, and it carries its own administrative and reporting framework.
Which federal agencies fund tribal programs?+
Tribal funding commonly flows from the Bureau of Indian Affairs, Indian Health Service, and HUD, among others, and often layers programs across agencies. Coordinating those streams into one coherent portfolio is a large part of the work.
How is compliance different for tribal governments?+
Tribes are sovereign governments, and federal awards still carry Uniform Guidance obligations, program-specific rules, and in many cases non-lapsing or self-governance provisions. Compliance has to respect both the sovereignty of the government and the requirements that attach to federal dollars.
